I mentioned this book to two of my 40 year old co-workers. I said “I’m reading a book about the importance of your twenties”. They looked a bit solemn and nodded. The staff developer said “**Yeah, your twenties really set the tone**” -- this is essentially the thesis of the book.

One of the most compelling arguments supporting that thesis is that we mostly stop changing past 30:

> there has been a spirited debate among personality researchers about whether people change after age thirty. Numerous studies have shown that, relatively speaking, we don’t. After thirty, our thoughts and feelings and behaviors remain incredibly stable.

Therefore, creating good habits in your twenties is important:

> Never again will we be so quick to learn new things. Never again will it be so easy to become the people we hope to be. So whatever it is we want to change about ourselves, our twenties are the easiest time to change it.

It's certainly up for debate which habits will actually help you be happier in the future. But the key take away, for me, is to be *intentional*.
